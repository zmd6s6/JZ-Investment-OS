## Objective

Deliver deterministic Portfolio, Risk, and PositionSizing behavior using only synthetic or
authorized de-identified inputs. The stage must preserve Core/Tactical separation, reserve pending
capacity before approval, prevent Risk Veto overrides, and create no Decision, approval, execution,
brokerage, or live-trading path.

## Deliverables

- [ ] Immutable Portfolio snapshots and Core/Tactical positions
- [ ] Deterministic portfolio-fit checks for single-name, sector, gross-exposure, cash, and pending
  capacity
- [ ] Versioned hard/soft Risk flags and immutable PASS/VETO assessments
- [ ] Pure versioned PositionSizing Engine with Decimal input hashes, lower-risk lot rounding, and
  binding-cap reasons
- [ ] Pre-approval capacity reservation and persistence mappings
- [ ] Synthetic normal, failure, boundary, and property tests for S3, S4, S6, and S7
- [ ] Stage evidence and project-status update

## Acceptance criteria

1. Core and Tactical quantities, costs, actions, and reasons remain separate; their quantities
   always reconcile to the total Position.
2. Every BUY/ADD capacity calculation accounts for current and pending exposure and cannot exceed
   single-name, sector, gross-exposure, or minimum-cash limits.
3. Hard Risk Veto has reason codes, Evidence references, expiry, and release conditions; it forces
   BUY/ADD sizing delta to zero and cannot be overridden by CIO or another component.
4. Position sizing is a pure, formula-versioned Decimal computation. Equal inputs return the same
   output and hash; lot rounding only reduces risk.
5. REDUCE/EXIT follows a separate risk-reducing path and is not blocked by negative new-risk
   capacity.
6. S3, S4, S6, and S7 are automated with synthetic fixtures. No path creates a Decision, approval,
   execution, brokerage request, provider credential, or live trade.

## Human decisions and out of scope

All coefficients and limits use existing `TEST_DEFAULT` policy configuration only. Choosing real
Portfolio inputs, real investment limits, final risk-code governance, any provider authorization,
or any execution mode requires human authority and is out of scope. Decision, approval, execution,
scheduler, UI, and Learning workflows remain owned by later stages.
